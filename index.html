Overview

The term "Chinese Revolution" is used in this project to cover key 20th-century transformations in China. The 1911 Xinhai Revolution ended Qing imperial rule and led to the Republic of China (Source 2). The later conflict between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) — together with the Japanese invasion — culminated in the CCP's victory and the founding of the People’s Republic of China in 1949 (Source 1). (Source 2; Source 1)

Timeline — Key Dates & Events

  1. 1894–1911 — Reform movements and revolutionary organizing before Xinhai (see Source 2).
  2. 1911 — Wuchang Uprising → end of Qing; Republic of China established (Source 2).
  3. 1927–1937 — KMT–CCP struggle; intermittent cooperation and conflicts (Source 9).
  4. 1937–1945 — Second Sino-Japanese War (context for later civil war; Source 1).
  5. 1946–1949 — Resumption of civil war; CCP victory and proclamation of PRC, Oct 1, 1949 (Source 1).

In-text citation examples: (Source 1) — secondary web summary; (Source 3) — primary photograph.

Causes (Concise)

  • Long-term weakening of Qing rule and foreign intervention; social unrest (Source 2).
  • Growth of nationalism and new political ideas (Sun Yat-sen's movement; Source 10).
  • Strengthening of organized political parties (KMT and CCP), later rivalry (Source 9).
  • Japanese invasion (1937–45) that shifted power dynamics and legitimacy (Source 1).
